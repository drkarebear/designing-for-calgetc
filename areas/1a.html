Academic writing across major assignments

Area 1A · English Composition

Academic writing, recursive writing processes, rhetorical awareness, substantial reading, feedback, and scaffolded college-level writing across major assignments.

At a glance

Cal-GETC Standard
  • One course: 3 semester or 4 quarter units.
  • Primary activity: college-level academic writing.
  • Writing minimum: at least 5,000 words of scaffolded writing appropriate for academic contexts across major assignments.
  • Process: major writing assignments receive formative peer and instructor feedback to support revision.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.1.1.

What the course must make visible

Cal-GETC Standard
  • A writing handbook or evidence of similar writing pedagogy, plus substantial reading.
  • Strategies for generating, drafting, and revising in multiple genres for multiple communities and audiences.
  • Formative peer and instructor feedback on major writing assignments to support revision.
  • Explicit attention to audience, purpose, context, genre, language conventions, and varied sources as evidence.
  • Academic genres such as synthesis-driven arguments, literature reviews, and analytical essays, with genre transformation also appropriate.
  • Main writing assignments that are not creative-writing genres.
  • At least 5,000 words of scaffolded writing appropriate for academic contexts across major assignments.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.1.1.

Does this course sound like a fit?

Faculty Design Note

Usually a good fit when…

  • The course is fundamentally about teaching college-level academic writing.
  • Writing is developed through recursive drafting, feedback, and revision.
  • Major assignments make rhetorical choices, source use, and genre visible.

Usually not a fit when…

  • The course is primarily literature or humanities content rather than composition.
  • The course is primarily creative writing or language acquisition.
  • The writing is designed mainly for a particular profession or major, such as journalism or business writing.

Faculty Design Note: these are plain-language screening cues drawn from the official area criteria, not separate approval rules.

Cal-GETC Area 1A

General-education standard

Requires at least 5,000 words of scaffolded writing appropriate for academic contexts across major assignments, along with recursive writing, rhetorical awareness, substantial reading, and feedback supporting revision.

ENGL C1000 CCN

Common-course requirement

ENGL C1000 separately requires a minimum of 5,000 words of formal writing across major assignments.

Faculty takeaway: A C1000 course has to satisfy both frameworks. Do not treat the broader Cal-GETC wording as replacing the more specific CCN language.

The Cal-GETC statement is from Version 1.4, §9.1.1. The ENGL C1000 statement is a separate CCN requirement and should be checked against the current official CCN template when the course is revised.

What might count toward the 5,000-word minimum?Faculty Design Note

Cal-GETC 1A names the quantity and character of the writing, but it does not give an assignment-by-assignment counting rule. For ENGL C1000, the CCN adds the separate requirement that the 5,000 words be formal writing across major assignments. A practical faculty discussion can start here:

Generally formal

  • Formal essays and extended academic writing
  • Research papers, literature reviews, and synthesis projects
  • Major reflective or metacognitive writing
  • Annotated bibliographies and research proposals
  • Substantial timed essays
  • Major public-facing academic writing
  • Substantial written components of multimodal projects

Depends on context / discuss locally

  • Peer review and peer-response writing
  • Discussion-board writing
  • Complete drafts of major assignments
  • Collaborative writing
  • Podcast/video scripts or other digital writing
  • Shorter analytical or reflective assignments

Usually informal / process writing

  • Journals and reading responses
  • Annotations and research notes
  • Brainstorming, freewriting, and outlines
  • Thesis, paragraph, citation, grammar, and similar skill exercises
  • Short reflections, exit tickets, and check-ins
  • Short-answer questions, quizzes, and brief in-class responses

Important: this classification is a faculty design framework, not an itemized statewide rule. Local curriculum faculty should decide how they interpret ambiguous cases for C1000.

Why Area 1B’s counting rule should not be imported into 1AUseful distinction

Area 1B explicitly says its 5,000-word total may include process drafts, written peer response, and other informal writing. Area 1A does not include that sentence. That difference does not itself settle every 1A gray area, but it is a reason to avoid assuming that every piece of process writing automatically counts toward the 1A or C1000 minimum.

Compare Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §§9.1.1 and 9.1.2.

What changed in Version 1.4?Version change

Version 1.4 replaced the prior 5,000-word formal-writing / 4,000 revised-final-draft formulation with the current requirement for 5,000 words of scaffolded writing appropriate for academic contexts across major assignments.

See tracked changes →

Gray area / useful nuance

Version 1.4 does not provide an item-by-item rule for whether discussion posts, peer review, drafts, journals, annotated bibliographies, or similar assignments count toward the 5,000. If a course also has a CCN template with additional writing language, read that requirement alongside Cal-GETC rather than treating the two as identical.
