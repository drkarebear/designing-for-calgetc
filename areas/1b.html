Critical reasoning through writing

Area 1B · Critical Thinking & Composition

Critical reasoning, inquiry-driven research, source evaluation, and argumentative writing that builds on Area 1A.

Explore the standards:
Check My Cal-GETC Area →
At a glanceCal-GETC Standard

One course · 3 semester or 4 quarter units.

Area 1A is prerequisite. Area 1B builds on rhetorical writing by making critical reasoning, inquiry-driven research, and argumentative writing major components of the course.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.1.2.

What the course must make visibleCal-GETC Standard
  • Instruction and practice analyzing, criticizing, and generating complex ideas.
  • Inductive and deductive reasoning, assumptions, logical errors or fallacies, and distinctions such as fact versus judgment.
  • Critical response to texts with attention to audience, purpose, and social context.
  • Evaluation of sources for relevance, reliability, and rhetorical appropriateness.
  • Analysis and construction of arguments in research and written work.
  • A minimum of 5,000 words of writing. The total may include process drafts, written peer response, and other informal writing that informs inquiry-driven research and writing.
  • Revision and instructor feedback on at least one extended argument.
  • Significant reading and analysis of written texts, a writing handbook or similar pedagogy, culturally aware text selection, and opportunities for reflection on learning and writing processes.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.1.2.

Does this course sound like a fit?Faculty Design Note

Usually a good fit when…

  • Critical reasoning and argumentative writing are major course components.
  • Students evaluate and construct arguments using research and source evaluation.
  • The course explicitly teaches reasoning processes, not just general analysis.

Usually not a fit when…

  • Critical thinking is only implied rather than explicitly taught in objectives and content.
  • The course focuses mainly on literary criticism without broader instruction in critical reasoning.
  • The writing component is too limited to demonstrate sustained argument and research.

Faculty Design Note: these are plain-language screening cues drawn from the official area criteria, not separate approval rules.

Make it visible in the CORReviewer Guidance

The statewide reviewer guidance emphasizes that reviewers should not have to infer missing criteria from the course title or discipline. Use the COR sections that best demonstrate the required content and rigor.

What changed in Version 1.4?Version change

Version 1.4 added and revised language to align Area 1B with UC transfer eligibility expectations. It now expressly says the 5,000 words may include process drafts, written peer response, and other informal writing, and it adds expectations for instructor feedback, substantial reading, cultural awareness, equity/access, and reflection.

See tracked changes →

Gray area / useful nuance

The standard is unusually explicit about what may count toward the 5,000 words in Area 1B. That wording should not automatically be imported into Area 1A, which uses different quantity-of-writing language.
