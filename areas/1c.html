Public speaking grounded in rhetoric

Area 1C · Oral Communication

Foundational public speaking grounded in rhetorical principles, research, ethical communication, live audience practice, and critical evaluation of public discourse.

At a glance

Cal-GETC Standard

One course · 3 semester or 4 quarter units.

The course provides foundational knowledge and practice in public speaking so students can communicate informative and persuasive ideas and critically evaluate the speeches of others.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.1.3.

What the course must make visible

Cal-GETC Standard
  • Theoretical foundations of creating and sharing knowledge, including rhetorical canons and ethos, pathos, and logos.
  • Finding, evaluating, and using primary and secondary supporting materials for credibility, accuracy, and relevance.
  • Arguments organized around a guiding thesis and adapted to audience, occasion, and context.
  • Ethical communication practices including truthfulness, accuracy, honesty, and reason.
  • Rhetorical sensitivity to diversity, equity, inclusion, belonging, and accessibility.
  • A variety of well-prepared, faculty-supervised, faculty-evaluated speeches delivered to a live audience.
  • Effective verbal and nonverbal delivery, management of communication apprehension, critical listening, constructive peer criticism, and analysis of public discourse.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.1.3.

Does this course sound like a fit?

Faculty Design Note

Usually a good fit when…

  • The course is centrally about formal public speaking and rhetorical principles.
  • Students repeatedly prepare and deliver speeches to a live audience.
  • Research, argument, ethics, delivery, and critical listening are integrated.

Usually not a fit when…

  • The course is primarily interpersonal, small-group, or career-specific communication.
  • Speaking activities are incidental rather than a substantial, faculty-evaluated component.
  • The course is mainly performance or presentation skills without rhetorical foundations.

Faculty Design Note: these are plain-language screening cues drawn from the official area criteria, not separate approval rules.

Reviewer Guidance

What changed in Version 1.4?

Version change

Version 1.4 clarified that the course must have a substantial focus on rhetorical principles and that discipline- or career-specific oral communication courses are not a good fit for Area 1C.

See tracked changes →

Gray area / useful nuance

Do not assume that an older or differently designated oral communication approval automatically establishes Area 1C eligibility. The current Cal-GETC criteria should be visible in the COR.
