Quantitative reasoning as the primary purpose

Area 2 · Mathematical Concepts & Quantitative Reasoning

Transfer-level mathematics, statistics, or another quantitative discipline with mathematics and quantitative reasoning as the primary purpose.

At a glance

Cal-GETC Standard

One course · 3 semester or 4 quarter units.

The primary purpose and content must focus on mathematics and quantitative reasoning, including students’ ability to develop, present, use, and critique quantitative arguments.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.2.

What the course must make visible

Cal-GETC Standard
  • A transfer-level course in mathematics, statistics, or another quantitative discipline.
  • Mathematics and quantitative reasoning as the primary purpose and content.
  • Student ability to develop, present, use, and critique quantitative arguments.
  • For statistics, a mathematical basis that includes probability theory, estimation, applications, and interpretation.
  • Transfer-level mathematical reasoning that reaches beyond high-school preparation.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.2.

Does this course sound like a fit?

Faculty Design Note

Usually a good fit when…

  • Quantitative reasoning is the intellectual center of the course.
  • Students construct and critique quantitative arguments, not merely perform procedures.
  • The level and content are clearly baccalaureate-transfer appropriate.

Usually not a fit when…

  • Symbolic logic or computer programming is the primary subject.
  • A mathematics-for-teachers course focuses mainly on elementary competencies or teaching pedagogy.
  • Quantitative content is supportive or incidental rather than the course’s primary purpose.

Faculty Design Note: these are plain-language screening cues drawn from the official area criteria, not separate approval rules.

Make it visible in the COR

Reviewer Guidance

The statewide reviewer guidance emphasizes that reviewers should not have to infer missing criteria from the course title or discipline. Use the COR sections that best demonstrate the required content and rigor.

Gray area / useful nuance

A sequence may qualify only when students are required to pass all courses in the sequence and the transferable content is equivalent to an approved Area 2 course.
