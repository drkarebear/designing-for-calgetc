Arts through history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism

Area 3A · Arts

Arts courses whose major emphasis integrates history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism rather than primarily technique, skills, or performance.

At a glanceCal-GETC Standard

One Area 3A course is required as part of Area 3.

Courses should have as their major emphasis the integration of history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §§9.3–9.3.2.

What the course must make visibleCal-GETC Standard
  • Substantial integration of history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism.
  • Analysis and appreciation of works of cultural and aesthetic importance.
  • A liberal-arts orientation rather than primarily skills training or performance.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §§9.3–9.3.2.

Does this course sound like a fit?Faculty Design Note

Usually a good fit when…

  • History, theory, aesthetics, and criticism organize the course.
  • Studio or performance material is studied through substantial historical, theoretical, aesthetic, or critical inquiry.
  • The course develops analysis and appreciation rather than primarily technique.

Usually not a fit when…

  • The course primarily teaches technique, skills, or performance.
  • Beginning drawing, painting, performance, or similar skills-building is the main activity.
  • History or criticism appears only as a small supporting component.

Faculty Design Note: these are plain-language screening cues drawn from the official area criteria, not separate approval rules.

Make it visible in the CORReviewer Guidance

The statewide reviewer guidance emphasizes that reviewers should not have to infer missing criteria from the course title or discipline. Use the COR sections that best demonstrate the required content and rigor.

What the standard emphasizesWhere faculty can make it visible in the COR
History / theory / aesthetics / criticism as major emphasisObjectives and content
Analytical engagement with artistic worksAssignments and evaluation
Liberal-arts rather than skills focusContent, methods of instruction, assignments

Reviewer Guidance + Faculty Design Note: the table suggests where evidence can be made visible; it does not create new state-mandated COR fields.

What changed in Version 1.4?Version change

Version 1.4 clarified the language excluding courses that focus on technique, skills, or performance from Area 3A.

See tracked changes →

Gray area / useful nuance

A performance or studio course is not automatically excluded. It can fit only when the major emphasis is the integration of history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism.
