At a glance

Cal-GETC Standard

Two courses · 6 semester or 8 quarter units.

Area 3 requires one Arts course (3A) and one Humanities course (3B). The overall area asks students to analyze and appreciate works of philosophical, historical, literary, aesthetic, religious, and cultural importance.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §9.3.

How Area 3 fits together

Faculty map
3A

Arts

The major emphasis integrates history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism. Technique, skills, or performance alone are not enough.

 3B

Humanities

Students analyze and appreciate works and ideas of philosophical, historical, literary, aesthetic, religious, and cultural importance.

Relationships faculty should notice

Cal-GETC Standard
  • Students need both 3A and 3B. A separate course is required for each subarea.
  • A course may be approved in both, but it can count only once.
  • The umbrella description is broad. The standards explicitly note that an individual course does not have to meet every element of the overall Arts and Humanities description.
  • Skills/performance is a recurring boundary. Arts courses centered mainly on technique or performance do not meet 3A; Humanities courses centered mainly on skills acquisition or performance do not meet 3B.

Source: Cal-GETC Standards v1.4, §§9.3–9.3.5.

A useful design question

Ask what the course is fundamentally asking students to do with the discipline. For Area 3, the strongest evidence usually shows sustained historical, theoretical, aesthetic, literary, philosophical, religious, or cultural inquiry rather than simply participation in an art form or acquisition of a skill.
